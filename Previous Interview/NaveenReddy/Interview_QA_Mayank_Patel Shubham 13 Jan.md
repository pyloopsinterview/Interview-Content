# Interview Q&A Summary

## 1. Why are you looking for a new role?
I’m not leaving because of anything negative. I’ve had a strong and successful run at Prudential Financial and taken an AI-driven platform from early design through production. What’s driving me now is growth and alignment—I want to work in an environment where AI-powered automation is core to the product, moves quickly from idea to impact, and gives me ownership to influence architecture and direction.

## 2. Where are you currently based?
I’m currently based in the Dallas–Fort Worth area, specifically Euless, Texas.

## 3. Are you open to in-person interviews or only remote?
I’m flexible. I’m open to remote roles, hybrid setups, and fully in-person interviews. There are no constraints on my side.

## 4. Are you willing to travel to New York or Los Angeles?
Yes, absolutely. I’m happy to travel to New York or Los Angeles for interviews or on-site meetings.

## 5. What was the hardest problem you dealt with at Prudential?
The hardest problem was taking AI-driven document processing from prototype to a trusted production system. This involved handling highly variable document quality, building safe AI orchestration with strict controls, and earning trust from claims operations through transparency and observability.

## 6. What was your team setup like?
The core team was about 6–8 people: backend engineers, data engineers, an ML engineer, and QA. I acted as the technical lead, owning architecture, production readiness, and mentoring, while working closely with product, operations, and compliance teams.

## 7. How did you work with your team?
I worked in a hands-on, collaborative way—setting clear goals, breaking work into well-owned components, pairing on complex problems, reviewing critical code, and acting as the bridge between engineering and business stakeholders.

## 8. Who made the key decisions on the hardest challenge?
I was the primary technical decision maker, responsible for architecture and engineering trade-offs. Business and risk constraints were set collaboratively with product, operations, and compliance, which I translated into technical solutions.

## 9. What were the major trade-offs in the project?
Key trade-offs included automation versus accuracy, speed versus safety, model complexity versus operational simplicity, and custom builds versus off-the-shelf tools. We optimized for long-term reliability and trust over short-term speed.

## 10. Were these trade-offs made in real time or hindsight?
Most major trade-offs were made in real time based on early pilots and risk signals. Some refinements came later from production feedback and hindsight.

## 11. What does your ideal role look like?
My ideal role involves owning AI-powered automation systems end to end, driving technical direction, staying hands-on, mentoring engineers, and building infrastructure that embeds AI into real workflows with measurable business impact.

## 12. How quickly could you travel for an in-person interview in LA?
With short notice, I could travel within a few days and realistically accommodate same-week travel.

## 13. How does your background align with a greenfield AI infrastructure role?
I have strong experience building AI platforms from scratch—designing APIs, orchestration layers, and tooling that enable internal teams to safely use LLMs. I enjoy high-ownership, greenfield work that delivers clear ROI.
