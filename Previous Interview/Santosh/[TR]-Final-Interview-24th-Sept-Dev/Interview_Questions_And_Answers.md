1. Can you tell me how your interview experience with SingleStone has gone so far? What did you think about your previous conversations with Ethan and Anthony?
 
 
2. Can you paint a picture of your role at Vanguard? Where did you sit within the organization, and what did the overall team structure look like?
 
 
3. Who were the different team members or teams you worked with at Vanguard, and what were their responsibilities compared with yours?
 
 
4. How did your role connect to the actual users and business stakeholders of the systems you were building?
 
 
5. How much of the work described on your resume was work that you personally owned versus work performed by the broader team? He specifically said he didn't like asking this simplistically and instead wanted to understand the organizational context around your contribution.
 
 
6. If another previous engagement is more representative of the type of consulting SingleStone does, can you use that project instead of Vanguard and explain it?
 
 
7. When you use the term “DevOps,” what does DevOps actually mean to you? He specifically contrasted configuration-driven platforms like Dynamics/Power Platform with traditional code-heavy environments.
 
 
8. What QA practices have you worked with on your recent projects?
 
 
9. Which QA practices did you find valuable and effective?
 
 
10. Which QA processes or practices have you seen that did not work particularly well, and why?
 
 
11. What SDLC or project-delivery methodology have you worked with recently? He was particularly interested in your Agile/Scrum experience.
 
 
12. Within Agile/Scrum, what practices or ceremonies have you found effective and supportive of delivery?
 
 
13. What parts of Agile/Scrum have you found less effective or more ceremonial than valuable?
 
 
14. What observability platforms or tools have you worked with? He gave Datadog as an example and asked about equivalent capabilities within Azure or Dynamics.
 
 
15. As an architect, how do you think about observability when designing a solution?
 
 
16. Imagine you walk into a client meeting on short notice and they want to evaluate their existing Dynamics architecture before moving to a new architecture. What non-functional requirements, or NFRs, would you tell them to prioritize?
 
 
17. How comfortable are you as a hands-on programmer?
 
 
18. If you weren't working as an architect and instead had to spend your entire day coding, would you be confident working as a full-time developer?
 
 
19. What is your experience with C# and writing code outside of Dynamics configuration?
 
 
20. How much experience have you had with AI-assisted code generation or AI-assisted software development?
 
 
21. What AI-assisted development tools have you used, and what do you think about them?
 
 
22. How do you see AI affecting the broader SDLC, beyond simply generating code?
 
 
23. What other Azure services have you used that we haven't already discussed?
 
 
24. Which Azure services or capabilities have you personally found particularly useful or interesting, and why?
 
 
25. You've talked about consuming APIs, but have you also written and published APIs that other developers or systems had to consume?
 
 
26. Imagine you've created a public or partner API and twelve external companies have integrated their applications with it. How would you design the API versioning strategy?
 
 
27. How would you manage the fact that your API has its own SDLC while all twelve external consumers have their own independent software-development lifecycles?
 
 
28. How would you evolve that API while providing the highest possible level of service to those external consumers? This was the question where he particularly liked your product-thinking approach rather than jumping immediately into URI versus header versioning.
 
 
29. Do you have any questions for me or anything else you'd like to understand about SingleStone or the role?
 
 
30. How do you feel about identifying additional opportunities within an existing client account? The discussion centered around his idea that “a full backlog is a funded backlog.”
 
 
31. How would you approach situations where you hear a client mention a legitimate business problem that is outside the current scope?
 
 
32. How do you think about balancing delivery of the current backlog with identifying additional valuable scope for the client?
 
 
33. How would you feel about a role where roughly 80% of your time might be client delivery and perhaps 20% could involve pre-sales or sales-support activities?
 
 
34. Would you be comfortable joining inbound prospect calls, gathering early requirements, and helping the sales team understand a potential Dynamics opportunity?
 
 
35. Would you be comfortable helping create the high-level architecture, scope, estimates, assumptions, and quote for a potential engagement?
 
 
36. How comfortable would you be working with other subject-matter experts during pre-sales instead of being expected to solution everything yourself?
 
 
37. How would you feel about occasionally context-switching away from your primary client to participate in several pre-sales opportunities during a month?
 
 
38. How would you feel about investing significant time in discovery, architecture, scoping, and estimation when the opportunity ultimately doesn't close?
 
 
39. Are you comfortable with the reality that several pre-sales opportunities might go nowhere despite the work you put into them?
 
 
40. How would you feel about helping sell or shape an engagement and then potentially having the opportunity to participate in delivering that same engagement?
 
 
41. How would you feel about this overall consulting dynamic being a regular part of your workflow at SingleStone?
 
 
 
The major themes of this R4 Director interview were consulting mindset, understanding your individual contribution within a larger team, DevOps, QA, Agile/SDLC, observability, NFRs, hands-on coding ability, AI-assisted development, Azure, API design and versioning, product thinking, identifying valuable client opportunities, pre-sales, scoping/estimation, and your comfort with the uncertainty inherent in consulting sales.
