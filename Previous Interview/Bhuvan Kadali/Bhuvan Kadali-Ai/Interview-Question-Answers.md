# Interview Questions & Answers

## 1. Tell me about yourself

**Answer:**
Hi, I’m Bhuvam. I’m a Senior Full Stack Engineer with around 12 years of experience, mainly working with React on the frontend and Node.js and Python on the backend.

I specialize in building scalable, production-grade applications, with a strong focus on clean architecture, maintainability, and performance. I’m comfortable working end-to-end across the stack—from developing responsive UIs in React to designing backend microservices and APIs.

In my recent role at Liberty Mutual, I’ve been leading development of a cloud-native claims processing and risk intelligence platform, where I worked on real-time data systems, performance optimization, and integrating machine learning for fraud detection.

---

## 2. What was the largest project you’ve worked on or ran yourself?

**Answer:**
One of the largest projects I worked on was at Liberty Mutual, where I led development of a cloud-native claims processing and risk intelligence platform.

This was a high-scale, real-time system used by claims analysts. On the frontend, I built a real-time dashboard using React that allowed users to track claims, view risk scores, and take action instantly, while optimizing performance for large datasets.

On the backend, I developed microservices using Node.js and Python and implemented an event-driven architecture to process high volumes of data efficiently. We also integrated machine learning models for fraud detection.

The system handled thousands of transactions daily, was deployed in the cloud with CI/CD pipelines, and significantly improved claim processing time and fraud detection accuracy.

---

## 3. Do you consider this project as successful or unsuccessful? What went well and what could be improved?

**Answer:**
Yes, I consider this project successful from both a technical and business perspective.

From a business standpoint, it reduced claim processing time and improved fraud detection accuracy by providing real-time visibility to analysts. Technically, we built a scalable cloud-native system using React, Node.js, and Python with an event-driven architecture.

What went well was strong team collaboration, clean architecture, and smooth CI/CD processes.

One area for improvement would be investing more upfront in performance testing and observability, as we initially underestimated some scalability challenges and optimized them later.

---

## 4. What was the hardest part of this project, and why?

**Answer:**
The hardest part was handling real-time data processing at scale while maintaining performance and low latency.

On the backend, designing an efficient event-driven architecture for high-volume data was challenging. On the frontend, managing frequent updates with large datasets without causing performance issues required careful optimization.

Integrating machine learning models into the real-time pipeline was also complex, as it required coordination across systems while ensuring fast and accurate results.

---

## 5. What is your proudest moment as an engineer?

**Answer:**
My proudest moment was when the real-time claims and risk intelligence platform went live and started being used by analysts.

It was rewarding to see how the system improved real-time decision-making and reduced manual effort. From a technical perspective, I was proud of building a scalable system using React, Node.js, and Python with real-time capabilities.

I was also proud of leading and mentoring a team of engineers and seeing them grow. The biggest satisfaction came from the measurable business impact—reducing processing time and improving fraud detection accuracy.

---

## 6. Tell me about a situation where you received constructive feedback on your work. How did you respond, and what did you learn from it?

**Answer:**
During the Liberty Mutual project, I received feedback during a code review that my backend design, while functional, might not scale well for high data volumes.

I took the feedback positively, discussed improvements, and refactored the system to follow a more event-driven, asynchronous approach. This improved scalability, performance, and fault tolerance.

From this, I learned the importance of designing for scalability from the beginning, being open to feedback, and collaborating early on system design decisions.
