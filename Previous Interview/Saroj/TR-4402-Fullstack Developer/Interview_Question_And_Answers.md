The work spanned multiple teams and domains, including billing, pricing, and downstream integrations. ---

## **5. Have you interacted with other teams, and can you describe a difficult cross-team technical decision you handled?**

Yes. One example was deciding how billing events should be persisted and replayed across systems. Different teams had conflicting preferences—some favored synchronous relational models, while others needed event replay and auditability. I facilitated discussions to understand constraints, documented multiple options, proposed an event-driven serverless approach using DynamoDB and S3, addressed concerns around observability and recovery, and validated the solution through a proof of concept. The decision aligned teams around shared outcomes like scalability and auditability.

---

## **6. How do you work with teams that have varying levels of expertise and confidence?**

I tailor my approach based on team maturity. I avoid jumping in and doing the work myself unless there’s a critical risk. Instead, I break problems into clear pieces, set expectations, review designs early, and provide guidance through design reviews and pair programming. My goal is to build confidence and capability in the team while maintaining delivery quality and timelines.

---

## **7. Do you work in Agile environments? What is your experience with Agile?**

Yes, I’ve worked in Agile environments for many years, primarily using Scrum or hybrid Agile models. I participate in sprint planning, backlog grooming, stand-ups, reviews, and retrospectives. As a lead, I help break down requirements, assess risk, provide technical input during planning, and remove blockers during execution. Agile helps maintain transparency, alignment, and continuous improvement.

---

## **8. How do you handle conflicts when team members disagree with your technical approach?**

I handle disagreements by keeping discussions objective and focused on trade-offs rather than opinions. I ask engineers to explain their approach, evaluate options against clear criteria like scalability, cost, maintainability, and risk, and document decisions. If the alternative is better, I adopt it. If not, I explain the reasoning clearly. When needed, I use proof-of-concepts to validate assumptions. Engineers are heard, but decisions are made decisively.
