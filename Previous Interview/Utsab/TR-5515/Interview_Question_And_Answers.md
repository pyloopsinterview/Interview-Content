# Interview Q&A – Utsab Poudel

---

## 1. Where do you stand? How do you see your profile fit for this role?

**Answer:**

I believe I’m a strong fit for this role, especially from a backend and architecture standpoint. I have 13 years of experience in backend engineering, primarily in Python, building production-grade APIs and microservices.

I’ve led the decomposition of a monolithic claims processing system into domain-driven microservices, which aligns well with the role’s expectation of building systems from scratch. I’ve worked on payment-related workflows, high-volume API systems, async processing, and internal service communication using gRPC.

Given the job description, I feel my experience in distributed systems, API design, and ownership of critical components aligns very well.

---

## 2. Is your experience more backend or full-stack/front-end?

**Answer:**

My experience is primarily backend-focused.

Most of my 13 years have been spent designing APIs, building microservices, implementing business logic, handling performance optimization, and ensuring production reliability.

While I understand frontend workflows and collaborate closely with frontend teams to define API contracts and error handling, my core strength and depth lie in backend architecture and distributed systems.

---

## 3. Have you worked with APIs in Python? Can you describe one or two projects?

**Answer:**

Yes, extensively.

One major example is at Health First, where I helped break down a monolithic claims processing system into Python-based microservices. I built REST APIs using FastAPI for services such as claims intake and payment calculation. These services handled high-volume submissions, so I implemented async endpoints for better throughput.

Another example is the eligibility validation service. It exposed APIs to validate insurance eligibility before payment calculation. For internal service communication where low latency was important, we used gRPC.

In both projects, I was involved in API design, business logic implementation, testing, and production monitoring — not just endpoint creation.

---

## 4. Have you used Django in any of your projects?

**Answer:**

I haven’t used Django extensively in my recent projects.

Most of my recent API development has been with FastAPI because it provides strong async support and high performance, which suited our microservices architecture.

That said, I understand Django’s architecture, ORM, middleware system, and request lifecycle. Since my core strength is backend system design in Python, adapting to Django would not be an issue if required.

In our case, FastAPI was chosen because we were building lightweight, high-performance services rather than full-stack monolithic applications.
